Reading the result

K–M treats martensite as athermal: the fraction depends only on undercooling below Ms, not on holding time.

The original α = 0.011 K⁻¹ came from high-carbon steels near 1 wt% C. Lower-carbon steels transform faster per kelvin, which the van Bohemen–Sietsma α captures. Lee & Van Tyne also fit an exponent n, so the curve is no longer a pure exponential; their fit covers 0.2–1.2 C, ≤2.1 Ni, ≤1.2 Cr, ≤0.3 Mo with about 0.75 Mn and 0.25 Si.

For Q&P, this gives the primary martensite at Tq. Fresh martensite on the final quench needs the carbon-enriched austenite's own Ms, which this page does not compute.

Composition-based Ms ignores prior austenite grain size, which lowers Ms in fine-grained or nanostructured austenite. Nb, Ti and V are usually tied up in carbonitrides, so enter only the amount in solution if you know it.
